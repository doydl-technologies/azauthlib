1. Scope and privacy roles

This Policy describes the standard azauthlib desktop application developed by DOYDL and the interactions it initiates. "Personal information" includes information that identifies or can reasonably be linked to a person, household, or device under applicable law. An identifier that is not a password may still be sensitive organizational information or become personal information when combined with other records.

Local processing is not the same as receipt by DOYDL. The App can handle profiles and secrets on your device without uploading those values to DOYDL. DOYDL does not acquire remote access to your files merely by publishing the App. Information you choose to send to support may be received by DOYDL and the communication providers involved.

Where DOYDL determines why and how information it actually receives is processed, it may act as a controller or equivalent responsible business. Where your employer or another organization determines how the App is deployed and how tenant information is used, that organization has its own obligations and may be the appropriate contact for your request. A software license alone does not establish a hosted processing service or a data-processing agreement.

Microsoft administers its identity services and account/tenant processing under the relevant Microsoft and organizational arrangements. GitHub, your browser, your operating system, and other destinations have separate practices. A modified build or another program embedding the azauthlib Python library may behave differently and needs its own accurate notice.

3. Where information comes from

The App receives configuration from values you enter, existing profile/configuration files, and the OS credential store. Authentication services return metadata, codes, tokens, and status information when you initiate a test. The local environment supplies display, filesystem, and network information needed to run the App.

The Python library can also accept credentials through an explicitly chosen environment file, environment variables, or direct caller input. Those developer-controlled paths are not a claim that the desktop App searches your entire disk or silently imports arbitrary files.

DOYDL receives support content from the sender through the communication channel used. A public repository handle may accompany an issue. This does not mean DOYDL routinely obtains your Microsoft account profile, browser history, local documents, or tenant directory contents.

4. Network requests and who receives them

4.1 Local legal documents

The desktop Terms of Use and Privacy Policy are included in the application package. The App reads those HTML files locally and prepares their text for native drawers during interface loading. It does not fetch website policies at startup, run website scripts, load tracking pixels, or send a legal-notice request to doydl.studio.

4.2 Tests you choose to start

When you choose Test connection, the App uses MSAL to communicate with the Microsoft identity platform. The desktop transport restricts its own identity HTTP requests to the Microsoft public-cloud host login.microsoftonline.com over verified HTTPS. It sends the configuration and authentication material required by the chosen flow. Your browser's sign-in journey may visit additional Microsoft, organizational, or federated identity destinations.

For client credentials, the token request includes the client secret. For interactive and device-code flows, account selection, password entry, multifactor checks, and consent are handled through the identity-provider experience. The current test requests Microsoft Graph's .default scope; permissions depend on your registration and consent.

Identity services and relevant network infrastructure receive ordinary connection information, including IP addresses, request times, and protocol metadata. A DNS resolver can see domain lookups; a managed organizational proxy may observe additional information under its own arrangements.

The test checks token issuance. It does not subsequently retrieve Graph mail, files, contacts, calendars, or directory contents. Other programs using the library and your credentials may do so under their own control and must provide their own disclosures.

5. Local storage and credential protection

Application data locations are selected using platform-specific user configuration and cache directories. On Windows, configuration normally resides under %LOCALAPPDATA%\azauthlib; cache and other operating-system paths can vary by platform or environment. A custom library token path may point elsewhere.

  • profiles.json records profile IDs, names, and the active selection.
  • The default .env and per-profile configuration files contain non-secret application values, such as tenant and client IDs and the authentication method.
  • ui_preferences.json stores display preferences, where the corresponding feature is used.
  • connection-test-results.json stores outcome records, fingerprints, and signatures rather than raw token responses or raw secrets.
  • The configured keyring backend stores client secrets under the azauthlib service, keyed by client ID. A separate azauthlib.connection-tests entry can hold the fingerprint key.
  • Known profile files removed through the App may be moved into a removed-profiles archive within configuration or cache storage.

Profile and preference files are not themselves represented as encrypted vaults. A tenant ID or client ID is generally an identifier, not a password, but can still disclose organizational relationships. Protect those files and their backups accordingly.

The strength and accessibility of secret storage depend on the actual keyring backend and OS account. The intended Windows backend uses the OS credential facilities; another platform or a custom backend may differ. The App reports a credential-store failure rather than deliberately writing the client secret into its public configuration file.

Other software with the appropriate account or administrative access may be able to obtain protected data. Disk encryption, endpoint controls, and a locked session remain important; this Policy does not guarantee protection against a compromised operating system.

6. Authentication information and tokens

The desktop Test connection implementation uses a fresh in-memory MSAL token cache for each test, disables MSAL's personally identifiable information logging option, and reports a summarized status rather than exposing a raw token in the interface. Public-client test flows explicitly exclude the offline_access scope.

Temporary tokens, claims, codes, configuration snapshots, and secrets may exist in process memory while work is performed. "Memory-only cache" does not guarantee cryptographic wiping, zero retention by the OS, or exclusion from swap, crash dumps, security software, or debugging tools. The App does not promise that every sensitive memory allocation is overwritten immediately.

The separate library behaves differently: callers of the azauthlib authentication library can use persistent MSAL caches that may contain account records and access, refresh, or identity-token material. Persistence uses OS-backed encryption by default and fails if secure persistence is unavailable unless a caller explicitly enables plaintext caching. A custom library integration needs to disclose the settings it actually uses.

Token expiry, consent, sign-in events, and revocation are controlled by Microsoft and the relevant organization. Closing the App or removing its local records does not guarantee immediate invalidation of an already issued token or deletion of Microsoft-side records.

7. Clipboard, browser, and device exposure

If you choose Copy for an active device-code sign-in, the App places that code on the system clipboard. The App does not automatically clear the clipboard afterward. Clipboard history, device synchronization, remote-desktop tools, and other applications may preserve or read the copied content according to their permissions.

Some notice text can be selected and copied. Any manual copying of a credential or configuration value outside the App creates another potential copy. Keep screenshots, screen recordings, shared displays, and support attachments free of sensitive values.

A browser sign-in may leave browser cookies and account sessions even though the App's token cache was temporary. Browser privacy settings and the identity provider's sign-out/revocation tools control those records, not the App's Remove connection button.

12. Removing information, uninstalling, and revoking access

Removing a connection is deliberately recoverable. It removes the active profile entry, archives known profile files, and keeps shared OS secrets. You can have zero active profiles, but that does not establish that no related files or credentials remain.

  1. Cancel a pending test and close applications using the affected profile before cleanup.
  2. Remove the intended connection in the App. Verify that you selected the right profile.
  3. Review the corresponding removed-profiles archives and any other authorized copies before deciding what to delete. The App does not certify secure erasure.
  4. Use the OS credential-management tools to review the specific client-ID secret and any App fingerprint-key entry. Confirm they are not needed by another profile or program.
  5. Review separate library caches, custom paths, backups, clipboard history, and browser sessions if complete local cleanup is your goal.
  6. If access must end, use Microsoft/tenant controls to revoke or rotate secrets, review consent, and terminate sessions where appropriate. Local deletion is not revocation.

Uninstallers, package managers, and manual source removal may leave user configuration and keyring data in place. Deleting a program folder alone is not a reliable method of removing all related data. Consult your administrator if the device, credentials, archives, or retention rules are organization-managed.

Do not send DOYDL your actual secret, token cache, or complete profile directory to request deletion. DOYDL cannot erase a local file or a Microsoft-controlled record that it does not possess; it can explain the relevant workflow and address information it does hold.

16. Security and incident reports

The App separates public configuration from keyring secrets, uses verified HTTPS for its desktop test transport, summarizes test errors, and uses keyed fingerprints rather than raw secrets in saved outcome records. OS-backed encryption is the default for persistent library token caches.

These measures have limits. Local account compromise, malware, administrative access, memory capture, a custom keyring backend, an explicitly plaintext library cache, a malicious dependency, or an improperly secured backup can expose information. The App is not represented as independently certified or immune to breach.

Console/QML warnings and host-application logging may include technical details such as paths or correlation identifiers. The App does not automatically upload its console output, but you or an organizational tool may capture it. Inspect and redact diagnostic material before sharing it.

Use the private reporting process in SECURITY.md for a vulnerability. For a suspected compromise of your tenant, account, or device, also notify the responsible administrator and rotate or revoke affected credentials. Where DOYDL has a legally reportable incident involving information under its responsibility, applicable assessment and notification requirements govern; notification deadlines are determined by applicable law, and no guarantee is made that an incident cannot occur.
